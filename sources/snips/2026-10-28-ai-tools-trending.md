### Snip 9: One lane per tool, a reviewer from a different company, and reviews capped at three rounds
- Source: Medium, Ruman Agarwal
- Episode/article: "My AI work stack, October 2026"
- Date: 2026-10-28
- Link: https://medium.com/@ruman/my-ai-work-stack-october-2026-7983dd0b308e
- Summary: A practitioner who ran real work through nine AI coding tools this year published his October 2026 stack and his rule: pick each tool for a lane, because one tool for everything costs you whatever that tool is worst at. In his stack, Claude Code builds and owns anything touching his live data connectors, Codex reviews everything Claude builds before it lands because a reviewer from the same vendor tends to share the author's blind spots, and reviews are capped at three rounds because after that the problem usually needs a decision from him, not another round. - Loop/workflow angle: the most copyable operating rules of the day, stated as limits. One lane per tool, a cross vendor reviewer, a three round cap, and a named thing each tool is never allowed to touch. That is a workflow policy a two person business can write in five lines.

### Workshop pick: Snip 1, the customer who brings their own agent
The richest end-to-end workflow in today's file is preparing for the customer who arrives with an agent's research in hand, because every small business will meet that customer this year and the preparation is all writing, no code. The loop is complete and teachable. Collect every policy you actually run by, refunds, cancellations, fees, timing, and exceptions, into one place. Write each one as a plain answer a stranger could quote, with the rule, the reason in one sentence, and the date it was last checked. Test it: ask your own AI the hard questions customers ask, and fix every place where the answer it finds contradicts what your team would say. Then set the response habit: cite the policy line, stand behind it or name the exception you are making and why, and log every challenge that exposed a contradiction so the page improves. The workshop is called Write Down Your Policies So Anyone Can Check Them. It carries today's other rules with it: start rights and approval limits set in advance (Snips 3, 4, and 8), and one lane and one owner per job (Snip 9).
