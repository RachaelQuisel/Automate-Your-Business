## 1. INSIGHT — Why Combinatorial DELs Help Machine Learning
- DNA-encoded libraries pool millions of molecules, then identify binders by sequencing attached barcodes.
- Combinatorial construction creates near-neighbor molecules, making local structure–activity comparisons unusually powerful for modeling.

## 2. ADVICE — Use Redundant Barcodes To Catch False Positives
- Treat DNA-binding proteins as a major source of false positives in DEL experiments.
- Give each molecule dozens of independent barcodes so anomalous enrichment reveals binding to DNA rather than the molecule.

https://share.snipd.com/snip/8426a104-3f32-442b-b647-81e25f2feb72

## 3. INSIGHT — Scale Across Proteins To Debug DELs
- DELs become more reliable when companies screen many proteins instead of optimizing one precious experiment.
- Repeated screens expose library quirks, manufacturing differences, protein-quality problems, and assay failure modes.

## 8. ADVICE — Train On Activity Cliffs To Teach Causality
- Build datasets around activity cliffs, where tiny molecular or protein changes produce large behavioral shifts.
- These controlled contrasts force models to learn causal interactions instead of memorizing familiar pockets or building blocks.
