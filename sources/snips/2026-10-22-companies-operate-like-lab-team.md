## 1. INSIGHT — Great Ideas Grow In Unexpected Places
- The best ideas rarely emerge from scheduled design sprints; they surface when curious people pursue side projects obsessively.
- Labs should create chemistry around passionate builders and stay alert for unexpected demos shared in hallways or after hours.

## 2. ADVICE — Track What Is Almost Possible
- Track what is almost possible, then act when a technical capability crosses into reality and matches a meaningful user problem.

## 11. INSIGHT — AI Will Not Erase Product Specialties
- AI expands people's ability to build, but it does not eliminate functional expertise; specialties remain each person's major while building becomes a supporting skill.
- PMs may adapt quickly because they routinely simulate other functions, but every role still contributes distinct judgment and craft.

## 12. ADVICE — Build A Rhythm Of Sprints And Recovery
- Name intense sprint periods explicitly, let people opt in or out, and schedule recovery and exploration seasons afterward.
- Google Labs uses post-event hacking periods to generate seeds for the next cycle rather than treating experimentation as unproductive downtime.

https://share.snipd.com/snip/b54ce93b-945b-48c6-89b5-621aa5c2bb75

## 13. ADVICE — Connect Frontier Teams To The Core Business

- Many corporate labs fail after three or four years because they generate interesting prototypes without a bridge to commercialization.
