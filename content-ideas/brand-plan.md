- **Visual style:** an original, hand-painted watercolor look: soft edges, warm light, cozy and whimsical scenes. It is inspired by the mood of Studio Ghibli films. It does not copy any Ghibli character, frame, logo or film scene.

- **Content sources:** Snipd captures, Rachael's own builds (airbnbai.rent, La Maison), and the course repo. No client data, client names or student names.

### Hooks
- The first line is the headline. It works when it is the only line someone reads.
- The hook speaks to the reader's situation or promises a quick payoff:
  - "If I got laid off from a company that cited AI, here's what I'd do this week."
  - "Give me one minute, and I'll improve your Claude Code experience immediately."
- Experiment reports open with the time spent: "I ran an agentic AI experiment for a month, and here is what I learned."
- A surprising claim, then a turn: "My most valuable AI agent does absolutely no work, and I mean that as the highest compliment."
- The 100-post analysis names further hook types, which I did not fetch as posts: the belief flip ("I used to think..."), the expensive mistake, the insider meeting, and the number tease.

### Sentences, line breaks and length
- One to three sentences per paragraph, with a blank line between paragraphs.
- Short lines are often stacked for rhythm: "This is the first skill I built." / "And it's the skill I use most often." / "*drumroll*".
- Length ranges widely. The reference post is about 45 words. The screenshot-skill and agent posts are several hundred words. The 30-day analysis reports a spread from under 80 words to over 350.
- She writes casually and with contractions: "honestly", "plz", "you're a maniac", "good god".

### Lists
- Numbered emoji lists (1️⃣ to 4️⃣) in the experiment post. Plain numbered steps in the screenshot post. Hyphen bullets in the layoff post. Check-mark bullets (✔️) in the agent post.
- A lead-in line before the list: "Here's what you're going to do:".
- Each item names exact tools, prices or numbers, for example "purchase at least the $20/mo Claude account".

### Emoji
- Functional, not decorative: list markers, an arrow or a row of arrows before the call to action (↓ ↓ ↓), a bell (🔔 or 🛎️) before a follow or link line, 🎉 at the payoff, and 🔑 👁️ for a two-line contrast.
- Hashtags are rare. The 100-post analysis counted 3 of 100.

### Humor
- Self-deprecating or wry asides in parentheses: "(Also still healing my retinas from the red carpet...those cameras flash at you nonstop, good god.)"
- Callbacks: the same post ends with "not even the scorched retinas."
- Short punchlines on their own line: "I love Toby." / "(Not that way.)"
- Playful named agents: her assistant agent Simon manages agents named after Friends characters (Monica, Chandler, Phoebe).
- A teasing aside inside instructions: "(and if it's your desktop, you're a maniac, change it)".

### First-person framing of AI news
- She reports what she built or tried, then what she learned:
  - "This was created using Lovable."
  - "this is my most used Claude Code skill by a landslide and easily saves me an hour a week."
- She is open about what fell short: "I'm glad I gave people value (daily AI news), but it didn't feel like me." and "None of the posts went mega viral."
- She credits people by name: "Inspired by a great post from Claire Vo."
- Small preferences make her sound real: "(I prefer Vercel.)"

### Calls to action
- A course or resource link after an arrow line: "↓ ↓ ↓ 🔔 If you're looking to build with AI, and not just use it, I just dropped my free 5-day course..."
- A soft invitation: "Imagine what problems you could solve." and "Let me know how it goes."
- On Instagram (snippet only): comment a keyword to receive a free resource, for example "comment VIBEWORK and I'll send it to you for free ⤵️".

### Recurring word choices
- From the fetched posts: "honestly", "literally", "here's what", "the boring bits of AI are some of the most important bits", "No coding required.", "build with AI, and not just use it".
- From the 30-day analysis only: "Let me tell you what I learned.", "This is an insane [X].", "Oh wow".

### Her own list of AI writing tells (useful as a banned list)
From her post: "It's not X, it's Y", colons in titles, paragraphs or bullets of equal length, "Ending a post with a weird question", "A parade of em dashes", and words like "harness" and "supercharge". These overlap with Rachael's rules (no em dashes, no filler words) and go into the banned list in Step 4.

### What fits Rachael, and what does not
- **Fits:** first-person build reports; exact tools and numbers; honest status ("the part I'm still working out"); credit to podcast guests; one native video carrying a short caption. Her layoff post also recommends building a public portfolio and writing posts such as "I'm a [job] and I just built an AI agent that [value]." That matches Rachael's goal of being attractive to hire.
- **Adjust:** sweeping hooks and words like "insane", "absolutely" and "Anyone can do it" (Rachael bans absolutes; see D6); heavy capitals and emoji (D4, D5); metaphors (D3).
- **Rachael's own material to draw on** (from airbnbai.rent, already recorded in `2026-10-07-series-of-loops/snipd-ideas.md`): 11 years hosting, 659 guest reviews, a 4.97 average rating, "Quisel rhymes with Ms. Frizzle", and the La Maison daily staff text.

### Delivery: "a secret with a best friend"
- Open by leaning in to the reader, with one short line before the reveal.
- Use "you" and "I", with no "we at airbnbai".
- Make one reveal per post, then show the proof: a screen clip, a number or a step.
- Keep the excitement in the specifics, not in volume: at most one exclamation mark, and no strings of capitals in the voiceover.
- End quietly with one next step for the reader.

## Appendix B: Visual style

### Look
An original, hand-painted watercolor storybook look:
- Soft washes, visible paper grain, gentle edges.
- Warm late-afternoon light.
- Cozy interiors and coastal hillsides.
- Small whimsical details, such as a tiny paper-lantern helper creature or plants on a windowsill.

Rules:
- No existing characters, studio names or recreated film scenes.
- No text painted by the image model. Titles and captions are added afterward with the brand fonts, so spelling stays correct.

### Palette (proposal for D11)
| Role | Color | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Paper / background | cream `#FBF8F3` | keep |
| Main ink, titles | deep aqua `#1D4E5B` | keep |
| Accent | aqua `#286575` | keep |
| Warm light | sand `#F3D9A4` | keep |
| Sky wash | soft blue-grey `#CFE3E6` | add |
| Hillside wash | sage `#B9CDA8` | add |
| Sunset wash | blush `#EBC3B5` | add |
| Line art | warm charcoal `#3E342E` | add (the same ink as her Ghibli Icon Maker / Soft Index skill) |

- Fonts: keep Manrope for headings and Source Sans 3 for body text.
- Caption boxes: cream at about 90% opacity with deep aqua text, so captions stay readable on painted backgrounds.
- Placement: keep the Instagram Reels safe zones already used in the module videos.

### How it gets made
1. Generate the background art with the tool chosen in D12, using one master style prompt plus the palette colors.
2. Save the approved images and reuse them as style references, so later images match.
3. Add text, captions and screen recordings with the existing ffmpeg and HTML build.
4. Formats: 1080×1920 for Reels and vertical video; 1080×1350 for feed images and carousels.
