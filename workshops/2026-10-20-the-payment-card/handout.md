### The three ideas (tape this to your monitor)

1. **Approve first, automate second.** The human decides. The agent carries out.
2. **A named agent with a cap beats a shared login with hope.** Its own name, its own limits, nothing else.
3. **Every payment leaves a receipt a person can read.** No receipt, no automation.

### The numbers that make the case

From this week's stories: Visa, BILL, and Billtrust launched a live pilot on October 20 where a registered agent pays approved invoices end to end through supplier portals, using a token instead of shared logins, with BILL serving nearly 500,000 businesses. The city of Raleigh reported cutting IT support costs by 66 percent and resolving nearly half of requests without a person, on a help desk team of four supporting about 4,400 employees. And a security firm reported that 65 percent of organizations had at least one incident caused by an agent on their network in the past year, which is why the cap, the list, and the stop are on the card.

### Homework (within two weeks, three payments)

Card your three most repeated payments. Run the next three payments of each kind by hand against the card, checking the five lines out loud. Mark any line you had to guess at, sharpen that line once, and highlight the one line you changed. Bring back the card. The changed line is the proof the card is real.
