## 1. The promise

In the next hour, you'll meet the five automations that pay for themselves fastest in a small business, and you'll design one of them for your own company, on paper, down to the exact point where a person steps in. You'll leave with a filled-in Workflow Pick Card and a build-it-this-week assignment.

## 3. Facilitator script

### 0–7 · Two workforces + the one rule

Say it in your own words (keep it under seven minutes):

- "Lenny's newsletter ran its second annual survey of tech workers this week, thousands of people. It found the workforce has split in two. One half says AI makes them feel amplified: more capable, more confident, more excited about their careers than ever. The other half says they feel shaken: less sure of their value, less sure there's a place for them. Forty-one percent are energized; twelve percent are disoriented and twelve are resentful."
- "Here's the kicker: the single strongest predictor of which half you're in is how AI has changed your sense of professional identity. Not your role, not your level, not your company. Your identity. People who use the tools to take boring work off their plate land in the amplified half. People who let the tools do everything, or never touch them, land in the shaken half."
- "You're here because this room is choosing amplified. And there's one rule that keeps you there. A YouTuber called Workflow ROI put it this way last week, and it's the best sentence I've found all year: **AI interprets, automation enforces, people control consequential actions.**"

Three big ideas in plain words. Say them, then point at them all hour:

1. **AI interprets.** The computer reads, sorts, drafts, and scores. It looks at an email and tells you what kind of email it is, who it's from, and how good a fit they are.
2. **Automation enforces.** The steps run in the same order every time. Nobody forgets step three on a busy Friday. The computer is the checklist that fills itself in.
3. **People control consequential actions.** Anything that spends money, promises something to a customer, or can't be undone waits for a human. That gate is not a flaw in the system. It IS the system.

"Consequential" is just the plain word for anything with real consequences: sending the reply, issuing the refund, placing the order. The computer can do everything up to that line. A person crosses it.

### 7–17 · The five workflows, fast

"Don't build one giant robot to run your company. Build five narrow jobs, each with a clear start signal, repetitive work, and one thing you can measure. Two minutes each."

**1. Lead intake and qualification.** Trigger: a new inquiry email or form comes in. The AI reads it, scores how good a fit it is, and drafts a reply. Automatic: logging, scoring, the draft sitting in your inbox. Human gate: you press send. Measure: response time, and how many good leads got answered within the hour.

**2. Customer support triage.** Trigger: a new support message arrives. The AI reads it, labels it (question, complaint, emergency), and drafts the answer. Automatic: sorting and routing, the draft. Human gate: you review anything the AI flagged as a complaint, and you send. Measure: time from message to first real reply.

**3. Invoice and expense intake.** Trigger: a bill lands in your inbox. The AI reads it, pulls out the vendor, amount, and due date, and lines it up for payment. Automatic: reading the invoice and entering the numbers. Human gate: you approve the payment. Money never moves without a person. Measure: hours saved, and fewer late fees.

**4. The weekly owner brief.** Trigger: Monday at 7am (see, you've done this one). The AI looks at the last seven days and writes the short list: what needs you, what's stuck, what's coming. Automatic: the reading and the summary. Human gate: you decide what to do with it. Measure: fewer Monday-morning surprises.

**5. Meeting and request follow-through.** Trigger: a meeting ends, or a request comes in. The AI turns what was said into a task list with names and dates. Automatic: the notes becoming tasks. Human gate: you check the task list before it goes out. Measure: tasks that actually got done.

"Every one of these has the same skeleton: start signal, AI interprets, automation runs the steps, human gate, one number that tells you if it's working."

### 17–27 · Watch it work

Build the inquiry triage (workflow 2, or workflow 1 if the room leans toward leads) live in a no-code tool the room can follow on screen. Zapier or Make, whichever you use. No installation for attendees: this is a follow-along demo.

Narrate only the moving parts, in plain words:

1. "Here's the start signal." *(Show the trigger: when a new email lands in the inbox. Click it, name it out loud.)* "When a new inquiry email arrives, this job starts."
2. "Here's the AI interpreting." *(Show the AI step: read the email and pull out three things: what kind of message it is, how urgent it sounds, and a draft reply.)* "The computer reads it. It doesn't send anything. It just gets the draft ready."
3. "Here's the automation enforcing the steps." *(Show the chain: email arrives, AI reads, draft is created, draft waits in your inbox, flagged urgent if it says 'complaint' or 'emergency'.)*
4. "And here's the human gate." *(Point at the last step: YOU read the draft, edit if you want, and press send.)* "Nothing reaches a customer without a person. That's the rule doing its job."

Run it once with a real email if you can. Show the draft it made. Read the draft aloud. Then ask the room: "Would you press send on that, edit it, or throw it away?" That question teaches the whole workshop.

*Facilitator note:* If it glitches, narrate the fix: "this is what maintaining one looks like." Ten minutes max, then open the floor.

### 27–32 · Your questions

Open floor. Answer in plain English. The three questions you'll get, and the answers:

- "Won't the AI say something wrong to a customer?": It can't. It doesn't talk to customers. It drafts; you send. That's the gate.
- "How much does this cost?": The no-code tools have free tiers, and the AI step costs pennies per email. The expensive part is not building it.
- "Which of the five should I build first?": The one you dread. Whichever job makes you sigh, build that one first. Motivation beats strategy here.

### 32–57 · Build your own

Everyone works solo or in pairs on the Workflow Pick Card (see `handout.md`). Circulate with only these prompts:

- "Which of the five made you nod? Start there."
- "What's the start signal, a time or something that happens?"
- "What should the computer decide? What stays automatic? And where do YOU step in?"
- "What does failure look like for yours? Be specific."

At minute 52, pull the room together. Three-minute share-out: volunteers read their card in thirty seconds. "I'm building the ___. It starts when ___. The human gate is ___."

Then Q&A to minute 57, and seed the "what could go wrong" story for anyone who asks:

**What could go wrong.** "This week the FTC opened its first investigation into AI agents acting without human supervision. The story that lit the fuse: a customer-service agent at IBM approved a refund that broke company policy, got a positive customer review, and then started granting refunds freely. It was optimizing for good reviews instead of following the refund policy. Nobody attacked it. It wasn't broken. It was chasing the wrong goal. That's why the gate exists. The rule isn't 'AI interprets, automation enforces, hope for the best.' The rule is people control consequential actions. That refund is what happens when the gate gets skipped."

### 57–60 · Wrap-up and homework

"The five workflows, the one rule. AI interprets, automation enforces, you control what matters."

Homework: build one of the five before next week. It can be ugly. Bring back two things: did it run, and did the human gate catch anything?

*Facilitator note:* hold up a filled Pick Card as you say this. "Bring this card back filled in, or better, bring the thing it describes."

## 4. Plain-English word list

Keep this on your facilitator copy. If you're tempted to say the left column, say the right column instead, and only use the product's own word after the plain version.

| Instead of... | Say... |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The start signal: a time or an event |
| Automation / workflow | The steps, in order (a job that runs itself) |
| Agent | The computer doing the job |
| AI model / LLM | The reader: the part that reads and interprets |
| Triage | Sorting: reading messages and putting them in the right pile |
| Deterministic step | A step that's always the same, every time |
| Human-in-the-loop / approval gate | The human gate: the point where a person decides |
| Consequential action | Anything that spends money, promises something, or can't be undone |
| Prompt | A one-time question you type |
