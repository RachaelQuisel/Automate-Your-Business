# Teach the Computer One Boring Job

**Source:** AI Tools Daily snips, 2026-10-01 — Snip 9 (Google replaces Gems with Skills) + Snip 10 (Gemini Spark scheduled automations)
**Snips file:** `sources/snips/2026-10-01-ai-tools-trending.md` — 13 snips, 2 used here, 11 held for later

**Teach date:** TBD
**Length:** 60 minutes
**Audience:** Small business owners and operators — no technical background assumed

## 1. The promise

In the next hour, you'll teach a computer to do one boring job for you — and set it up so it keeps doing it every week without being asked. You'll leave with a one-page recipe card for that job, and you'll know how to make more.

## 2. Agenda — 60 minutes

| Minutes | Segment |
|---------|---------|
| 0–5 | The one big idea |
| 5–15 | Watch it work (live demo) |
| 15–20 | Your questions |
| 20–45 | Build your own (independent work) |
| 45–55 | Show and tell + questions |
| 55–60 | Wrap-up and homework |

25 minutes of the room building, 15 minutes of questions. The talking parts are short on purpose.

## 3. Facilitator script

### 0–5 · The one big idea

Say it in your own words — keep it under five minutes:

- "You know those jobs you do over and over — checking email, sorting photos, writing the same summary every Monday? Imagine writing down the instructions once, like a recipe card, and the computer follows them forever. That's all a 'skill' is: a saved set of instructions."
- "And a 'trigger' is just what tells the computer when to start — like an alarm clock. Every Monday at 7am. Or: when a new email arrives."
- "This week, Google gave every free Gemini user exactly this. You write the instructions once, and it runs on its own. That's the whole workshop."

Three lines the room should remember:

1. Write the instructions once.
2. Tell the computer when to start.
3. Start with the most boring job, not the fanciest one.

### 5–15 · Watch it work

Build the Monday Morning Brief live in Gemini. Narrate only three steps, then stop talking:

1. "Here's the instruction I'm saving." *(Read it aloud as you type it: "Every Monday at 7am, look at my emails from the last 7 days I haven't answered and my calendar for this week, and make me a short list: who needs a reply, what each meeting needs, and anything that looks stuck.")*
2. "Here's when it runs — every Monday at 7am."
3. Run it once. Show the result on screen.

*Facilitator note:* If it glitches, don't panic — say "this is what fixing looks like" and fix the wording live. Ten minutes max, then open questions.

### 15–20 · Your questions

Open floor. One rule for yourself: answer in plain English. If you catch yourself saying "API" or "agentic workflow," restart the sentence.

### 20–45 · Build your own

Everyone works solo or in pairs on the Loop Card (see `handout.md`). You circulate. The only prompts you need:

- "What's the job you dread repeating?"
- "When should it start — a day and time, or when something happens?"
- "Say the instructions out loud to me, like I'm the new hire."

By minute 45, everyone should have three things written down: one job, one start signal, and the instructions in plain words.

### 45–55 · Show and tell

Volunteers read their recipe card in under a minute each: "Every ___, my instructions do ___."

Then questions — especially "what could go wrong?" Give the three honest answers in plain English:

1. **Instructions go stale.** Re-read yours once a month, or the computer keeps doing last month's job with total confidence.
2. **Nobody's watching.** Pick a person who'll notice if it stops running. Jobs that run quietly fail quietly.
3. **The computer doesn't decide.** It does the assembling; you do the deciding. Never hand it the apology, the refund, or anything that needs judgment.

### 55–60 · Wrap-up and homework

Homework: set up your one job before next Monday. Bring back two things — did it run, and what did you change about the instructions after seeing it run once?

*Facilitator note:* the workshop is itself the pattern. The Loop Card is the saved instructions; "bring it back next week" is the start signal.

## 4. Plain-English word list

Keep this on your facilitator copy. If you're tempted to say the left column, say the right column instead — and only use the product's own word after the plain version.

| Instead of... | Say... |
|---|---|
| Skill | Saved instructions (like a recipe card) |
| Trigger | The start signal — a time or an event |
| Automation / loop | A job that runs itself |
| Agent | The computer doing the job |
| Prompt | A one-time question you type |
| Workflow | The steps, in order |
