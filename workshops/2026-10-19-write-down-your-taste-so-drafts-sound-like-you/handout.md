### The three ideas (tape this to your monitor)

1. **Mechanics are not taste.** A perfect cut can still miss the joke.
2. **Taste you cannot write down, a tool cannot follow.** Sharpen every rule until it can be checked.
3. **The human job moves, it does not vanish.** You judge drafts instead of rebuilding them.

### The numbers that make the case

From this week's stories: one tool, Claude Code, orchestrated three others, FFmpeg, Whisper, and Python, to turn phone footage into captioned shorts, and the creator named teaching it funny, not making the edit, as the hard part. A second tool, Reely, produced a draft reel in 7 to 20 minutes in its author's tests, against 25 to 50 minutes by hand in CapCut. A production guide this week priced extra steps plainly: at 90% success per step, a 3-step job lands at 72.9% and an 8-step job at 43%, so keep the loop short.

### Homework (within two weeks, three drafts)

Run your loop three times on the same job with your Taste File. Mark each draft keep, cut, or sharpen. Update the file once from what you learned and highlight the one rule you changed. Bring back the updated file. The changed rule is the proof the system is learning your taste.
